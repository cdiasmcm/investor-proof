# The Perfect VC Pitch — consolidated learnings

Built from a review of 23 early-stage startup pitch decks from an MBA entrepreneurship course,
checked against common venture-capital pitch deck frameworks and a score-times-confidence approach
to startup assessment. The machine-readable version the agent uses is [`rubric.json`](rubric.json).
This page is for people.

**A perfect pitch scores 100%:** level 4 (investor-grade) on all 13 criteria.

## How scoring works
Each criterion gets a level from 0 to 4. Level → share of the weight: 0 → 0%, 1 → 25%, 2 → 50%,
3 → 75%, 4 → 100%. The total is the sum, out of 100.

**Evidence cap:** a claim with no data, source, named customer or document behind it can't score
above level 2. Investors discount gut feel, so confidence is part of the score.

## The 13 criteria and their weights

| # | Criterion | Weight | What level 4 looks like |
|---|-----------|-------:|-------------------------|
| C01 | Company purpose (the hook) | 5 | One sentence: what, for whom, outcome. Said in the first 30 seconds |
| C02 | Problem | 9 | Specific customer, quantified pain, today's workaround and why it fails |
| C03 | Solution and how it works | 7 | Value proposition mapped to the pain; concept flow or demo; a before/after use case |
| C04 | Customer validation and traction | **12** | Interview/survey counts, attributed quotes, LOIs, pilots, revenue; what you learned |
| C05 | Market size and target customer | 8 | TAM top-down, SAM/SOM bottom-up with sources; beachhead and why; persona |
| C06 | Why now | 3 | Two or more sourced trends that make the timing right |
| C07 | Competition and defensible advantage | 8 | Named competitors incl. status quo; honest comparison; a moat that's hard to copy |
| C08 | Business model and unit economics | **12** | Who pays what, how often; validated pricing; margin per unit; CAC and LTV |
| C09 | Go-to-market, launch and growth | 9 | Funnel (AIDA) with channels; beachhead and first 90 days; adoption pace; 36-month plan |
| C10 | Team and advisors | 7 | Role + relevant experience per founder; advisors who fill named gaps |
| C11 | Financials and assumptions | 7 | 3-year P&L and cash flow; sourced assumptions; break-even; sensitivity; consistent numbers |
| C12 | The ask, use of funds, investor return | 7 | Exact amount and terms; use of funds to a milestone; exit paths and likely acquirers |
| C13 | Delivery and storytelling | 6 | Clear arc ending in a call to action; ~10 minutes; precise, confident, no filler |
| | **Total** | **100** | |

Why these weights: validation and the business model carry the most because they varied most
across the reviewed decks, and because they answer the two questions investors ask first: do
people really care about this problem, and can this become a profitable, fundable business?

## What the reviewed decks taught

**What the strongest pitches did**
- **Counted, attributed validation.** They said how many customers they interviewed or surveyed,
  quoted them by role across more than one customer type, and showed commitments such as letters
  of intent, pilots or committed partners.
- **Unit economics as a worked example.** They walked one typical transaction through price, cost
  and margin, or gave a lifetime-value formula, contribution margin and break-even month.
- **A chosen beachhead.** They picked a narrow first market using data and explained why it beats
  the alternatives.
- **Assumptions and sensitivity.** They listed each financial assumption with its source and
  showed what happens with fewer customers or lower retention.
- **A phased launch.** They broke the plan into 0–90 days, 3–6 months, 6–12 months and 1–3 years,
  each with a customer target.
- **A memorable hook.** An "X for Y" analogy or a short slogan made the concept easy to repeat.

**Where most pitches lost points**
- **Exit missing.** Only about 5 of 23 named an exit or likely acquirer.
- **No CAC or LTV.** Only 6 of 23 stated a customer acquisition cost and 2 a lifetime value; only 3
  showed a sensitivity case.
- **Competition grids where the startup wins every row**, and moats that are easy to copy.
- **Top-down market only.** Several jumped from a national TAM to a share with no bottom-up SOM.
- **Thin or inconsistent numbers.** Prices or margins that contradicted each other, very low
  margins left unexplained, and several decks with no break-even.
- **Problem without a workaround.** Many described the pain but not how customers cope today.
- **Team titles without relevance.** Executive titles listed with no link to what the venture needs.

## Not scored from a transcript
Slide design and live presence (including Q&A) matter in a real pitch, but neither shows in a
transcript. The agent scores content and delivery only and says so in every report.
